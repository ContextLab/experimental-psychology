# A synthesis is not (just) a summary

<div class="definition-box" data-title="Two very different things">

- **Summary:** "Smith et al. (2018) found X. Jones et al. (2020) found Y. Lee et al. (2022) found Z."
- **Synthesis:** "Together, these studies suggest *X is true under condition A but not condition B* &mdash; consistent with the broader idea that..."

</div>

<div class="warning-box" data-title="A discussion section that is just a list of summaries is a bad discussion section">

The reader can already see that you read the papers. What they want from you is the *connective tissue* &mdash; how the findings fit together (or don't), and what they collectively imply.

</div>

---

<!-- _class: scale-80 -->

# When studies seem to disagree, first ask: *do* they?

<div class="warning-box" data-title="Most apparent disagreements aren't real">

- **Different methods.** A self-report study and an fMRI study might both be "right" but measure different things
- **Different operationalizations.** "Memory" could mean recall, recognition, or working memory &mdash; pick a different one and you'll get a different result
- **Different populations.** College undergrads vs. children vs. older adults vs. clinical samples
- **Different effect sizes, not different signs.** Both papers show $r > 0$, but one rounds to "no relationship" and the other to "strong relationship"
- **Sampling noise.** With small $N$, two studies of the *same* effect can look very different just by chance

</div>

<div class="tip-box" data-title="When it's a real disagreement">

A genuine disagreement is when both studies measured the same thing the same way in comparable samples and got incompatible results. That's *interesting* &mdash; and worth saying so.

</div>

# Example: do these papers agree or disagree?

<div class="note-box" data-title="Three hypothetical papers on caffeine and memory">

- **Paper A** (Smith, 2019; *N* = 40 undergrads): Caffeine improves recall on a word list ($d = 0.6$, $p = 0.02$).
- **Paper B** (Jones, 2021; *N* = 200 adults aged 18&ndash;75): No effect of caffeine on a recognition task ($d = 0.05$, $p = 0.51$).
- **Paper C** (Lee, 2023; *N* = 80 sleep-deprived medical residents): Caffeine *strongly* improves working memory ($d = 1.1$, $p < 0.001$).

</div>

<div class="example-box" data-title="Think-pair-share">

- Do these three papers **agree**, **disagree**, or address **different questions**?
- Which differences (sample, task, measure) might explain the pattern?
- If you had to write *one sentence* synthesizing all three, what would it say?

</div>

# Building the narrative thread

<div class="tip-box" data-title="Concrete moves that make a synthesis work">

- **Frame the question first**, before introducing any paper. Readers need to know what they're trying to learn before they hear about the evidence
- **Group papers by what they tell you**, not by author or date. ("Three studies suggest X..." is better than "Smith found X. Then Jones found X. Then Lee found X.")
- **Name the tension explicitly.** "These studies disagree on whether X causes Y. The disagreement appears to come from..." is much stronger than ignoring it
- **Take a position.** What do *you* think the evidence actually shows? Hedge where appropriate, but don't refuse to commit

</div>

---

<!-- _class: scale-90 -->

# Being honest about uncertainty

<div class="important-box" data-title="Honesty is not the same as hedging everything">

- Be specific about *what* is uncertain: the direction of an effect? Its size? Whether it generalizes? The underlying mechanism?
- Distinguish **what the evidence supports** from **your speculation**. "These results are consistent with..." vs. "We speculate that..."
- Identify **testable predictions**. "If our speculation is correct, then we would expect to see X in a future study."

</div>

<div class="warning-box" data-title="The cop-out to avoid">

"More research is needed" is true of literally every result ever published. If you write it, follow it with *what kind* of research, and *why* that research would resolve the specific uncertainty you've named.

</div>
