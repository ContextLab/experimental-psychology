# Literature Review Lab Rubric (10 points total)

## Criterion 1: Template paper and citing/non-citing collection (1 point)

**Description:** Your group must have selected a single "template" paper (1996–2015, peer-reviewed, ≥25 citations, empirical, ~20 pages or shorter, psychology- or neuroscience-related, not retracted), 5 citing papers (2016 or later, peer-reviewed, no co-author overlap, with at least one replication attempt, one follow-up study, and one review paper), and 1 non-citing related paper (2016 or later, no co-author overlap, similar question or paradigm). Provide the bibliographic information for all 7 papers in the report (e.g., a numbered or bulleted list with author, year, title, journal).

| Points | Rating | Description |
|-|-|-|
| 1 | Exceeds | All 7 papers listed with full bibliographic information. The selection clearly meets all stated constraints, including the requirement that the citing-paper set contains a replication attempt, a follow-up, and a review. The non-citing paper is genuinely related (similar paradigm or question) — not just topically adjacent. |
| 0.5 | Below | All 7 papers present but at least one constraint is missed (e.g., template paper is from 2014, no replication attempt in the citing set, non-citing paper is too tangentially related, or one paper is by the same author as the template). |
| 0 | No evidence | Fewer than 7 papers, or papers not clearly identified, or major constraint violations (e.g., template is a review paper rather than empirical). |

---

## Criterion 2: Summary of the template study (1 point)

**Description:** Open the discussion section by summarizing the question, approach, and key findings of the *original* template paper. The summary should be tight (one paragraph) but precise — it should give a reader who hasn't seen the template paper enough to understand the rest of your discussion.

| Points | Rating | Description |
|-|-|-|
| 1 | Exceeds | Summary names the specific question being addressed, the experimental approach (paradigm, manipulations, sample), and the key result(s) with enough specificity that a reader who hasn't seen the paper could follow the rest of your discussion. Avoids quoting the abstract verbatim. |
| 0.5 | Below | Summary is present but generic — doesn't name the specific paradigm, doesn't describe the actual results, or relies heavily on abstract-style boilerplate ("the authors investigated X and found interesting results"). |
| 0 | No evidence | No summary of the template paper, or the summary is too vague to convey what was actually done. |

---

## Criterion 3: Engagement with related work (2 points)

**Description:** For each of the 6 other papers (5 citing + 1 non-citing), describe how it relates to the template paper. Compare and contrast: similar or different approaches, similar or different findings, similar or different interpretations. The discussion should *use* the related work, not just list it.

| Points | Rating | Description |
|-|-|-|
| 2 | Exceeds | All 6 related papers are engaged with substantively. The writeup names specific points of agreement or disagreement (not just "this paper also looked at X"), distinguishes replication attempts from follow-ups from reviews from alternative approaches, and articulates *what each paper adds* to the picture. The non-citing paper is used to provide an alternative framing or paradigm — not just listed alongside the citing papers. |
| 1.5 | Mastery | All 6 papers discussed, with most receiving substantive engagement. May lean too heavily on one or two papers and treat others perfunctorily. The non-citing paper is included but not clearly used to *contrast* with the citing-paper perspective. |
| 1 | Near | Three to five papers discussed substantively; the others are mentioned in passing or grouped together ("several studies also found similar effects"). |
| 0.5 | Below | Only one or two papers engaged with substantively. The rest are listed without commentary or grouped into vague generalizations. |
| 0 | No evidence | No engagement with the related work, or the related work is not actually integrated into the discussion. |

---

## Criterion 4: Synthesis and "true answer" interpretation (2 points)

**Description:** Draw conclusions about what you think it all *means*. Given everything you've read, what's your best current interpretation of the original question? This is the time-traveling part — you have the benefit of hindsight that the template paper's authors didn't have. Use it. The interpretation should be supported by evidence from across the papers, not just personal opinion.

| Points | Rating | Description |
|-|-|-|
| 2 | Exceeds | Articulates a clear, defensible interpretation of what the field's collective evidence (template + citing + non-citing) suggests about the original question. Explicitly uses hindsight: e.g., "the original interpretation was X; in light of [paper A] showing Y and [paper B] failing to replicate Z, the more defensible interpretation today is...". Considers and addresses at least one alternative interpretation, explaining why the chosen one is preferred. |
| 1.5 | Mastery | A clear interpretation is offered with supporting evidence from multiple papers. May not explicitly engage alternative interpretations or may lean more on personal reasoning than on the literature. |
| 1 | Near | An interpretation is given but it's either (a) just a restatement of the original paper's interpretation with no use of hindsight, or (b) supported only by general claims rather than specific findings from the related work. |
| 0.5 | Below | The interpretation is vague ("the answer is complicated") or hedged so heavily that no real position is taken. |
| 0 | No evidence | No interpretation given, or the writeup never gets past summarizing individual papers. |

## Criterion 5: Future directions (1 point)

**Description:** Identify still-unanswered questions raised by your reading and propose how they might be studied in future work. The questions should be *genuinely* open (not "more research is needed on X"-type filler), and the proposed approach should be specific enough that someone could conceivably design a study from it.

| Points | Rating | Description |
|-|-|-|
| 1 | Exceeds | Names at least one specific open question that your reading actually surfaced (e.g., "the citing papers all used adults; whether the effect holds in children is open"). The proposed study has enough detail to be evaluable: paradigm, key manipulation, what would count as evidence one way or the other. |
| 0.5 | Below | A future direction is mentioned but it's generic ("more studies should examine this") or the proposed approach is hand-wavy. |
| 0 | No evidence | No discussion of future directions, or the section is a single placeholder sentence. |

## Criterion 6: Bibliography (0.5 points)

**Description:** Include a bibliography listing all 7 papers (template + 5 citing + 1 non-citing), formatted consistently in any standard citation style (APA, MLA, Chicago, etc.). Each entry should include enough information to find the paper (authors, year, title, journal/venue).

| Points | Rating | Description |
|-|-|-|
| 0.5 | Complete | Bibliography lists all 7 papers with consistent formatting. Each entry has enough information to locate the paper. |
| 0.25 | Partial | Bibliography is present but missing entries, or formatting is inconsistent enough that it's hard to tell what's a paper vs. a journal vs. a publisher. |
| 0 | Missing | No bibliography, or fewer than 7 papers listed. |

Include the actual prompt(s) you used and at least one specific example of where the AI got something right and one where it got something wrong (or made something up).

| Points | Rating | Description |
|-|-|-|
| 1.25 | Exceeds | Documents the actual prompt(s) used. Includes a *verified* example of an AI mistake — e.g., "the AI cited Smith et al. Describes the verification workflow (downloaded PDFs, checked DOIs, etc.). Reflects on when AI deep research is useful and when it's dangerous. |

| 0 | No evidence | Deep research not mentioned, or mentioned only in passing without reflection. |

## Criterion 9: Writing quality and structure (0.25 points)

**Description:** The discussion section reads like a real discussion section — coherent paragraphs, transitions between points, a clear narrative thread from "here's what the original paper claimed" to "here's what we now think." Mechanics (grammar, spelling, citations in-text) are clean.

| Points | Rating | Description |
|-|-|-|
| 0.25 | Complete | Reads as a coherent piece of writing with smooth transitions. Mechanics are clean enough not to distract. |
| 0 | Missing | Writing is fragmented (bullet lists where prose is expected), or mechanical issues (typos, broken citations) make the writeup hard to follow. |

## Grading notes for TAs

- **AI-proofing**: The strongest AI-proof elements are the synthesis (Criterion 4), the GenAI deep research reflection (Criterion 7), and the NotebookLM activity (Criterion 8). Pay special attention to whether the writeup names *specific* findings, *specific* paper details, and *specific* AI errors that a student would only know if they had done the work.
- **Hindsight**: For Criterion 4, the test is "did the student actually use the post-2015 papers to update the original paper's interpretation, or did they just restate the original?" If it's the latter, the time-traveling premise of the lab is missed.
- **Bibliographic verification**: For Criterion 1, spot-check at least one citing paper against the template paper's "cited by" list (e.g., on Google Scholar). If it doesn't actually cite the template, that's a constraint violation.
- **Specificity over length**: A concise discussion that names specific papers, specific findings, and specific contradictions is worth more than a long writeup that speaks in generalities. The "could this have been written by someone who never read these papers?" test applies here too.
- **Non-citing paper**: For Criterion 3, the non-citing paper is meant to provide a contrasting frame. If the writeup treats it identically to the citing papers (no acknowledgment that it represents an "alternative" perspective), the student missed the point.
